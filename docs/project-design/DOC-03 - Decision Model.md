# Document 3 - Decision Model

# 1. Purpose of the Document

This document defines the decision model for the job search automation.

Its purpose is to establish the principles, rules, criteria, mechanisms, and constraints that will govern all decisions made during the processing of job postings, ensuring that those decisions are consistent, reproducible, traceable, and auditable.

This document defines how the automation will evaluate the available information, apply business rules, assign priorities, resolve anticipated situations, and determine when a decision can be executed automatically or will require user intervention.

It also serves as the official reference for the design, implementation, validation, and evolution of the system's decision engine, ensuring that all decisions remain aligned with the Functional Requirements, Non-Functional Requirements, and the General Principles defined for the project.

The provisions contained in this document shall be mandatory for all modules that perform evaluation, classification, prioritization, rejection, recommendation, or any other activity involving decision-making within the automation.

---

# 2. Decision Model Principles

The following principles establish the conditions that shall be satisfied by every decision made by the automation during the processing of job postings.

These principles complement the project's functional and non-functional requirements and constitute the mandatory criteria for the design, implementation, validation, and evolution of the decision engine.

---

### DMP-001. Rule-Based Decisions

Every automated decision shall be based exclusively on business rules that have been previously documented and approved.

Decisions based on implicit, random, or undocumented criteria shall not be permitted.

---

### DMP-002. Consistency

Given the same inputs, configurations, and business rules, the automation shall produce the same decision.

Decisions shall be deterministic and avoid inconsistent behavior.

---

### DMP-003. Reproducibility

Every decision shall be reproducible using the same information, configuration, and version of the business rules in effect at the time of execution.

---

### DMP-004. Traceability

Every decision shall preserve the information required to reconstruct the process that led to the obtained result.

Traceability shall include the evaluated data, the applied rules, the generated result, and the party responsible for the decision.

---

### DMP-005. Auditability

Every automated decision shall be supported by objective evidence recorded by the system.

The automation shall make it possible to determine what decision was made, when it occurred, why it occurred, and which rules supported the outcome.

---

### DMP-006. Separation Between Evaluation and Decision

The evaluation process of a job posting and the decision derived from that evaluation shall remain conceptually independent.

The evaluation produces objective information; the decision uses that information to determine the corresponding action.

---

### DMP-007. Controlled User Intervention

Strategic, personal, legal, or user representation decisions shall require the user's explicit approval before execution.

The automation shall not replace the user's judgment in these types of decisions.

---

### DMP-008. Integrity First

Whenever uncertainty, insufficient information, or conflicts between rules exist, the automation shall prioritize processing integrity and avoid decisions that could compromise the quality of the results.

When appropriate, it shall request user intervention or apply the strategies defined for such situations.

---

### DMP-009. Technology Independence

Decision rules and criteria shall be defined independently of the technology used to implement them.

Their validity shall not depend on any specific programming language, tool, provider, or artificial intelligence model.

---

### DMP-010. Controlled Evolution

Any addition, modification, or removal of decision rules shall be documented in advance and preserve compatibility with the rest of the decision model, avoiding inconsistent or contradictory behavior.

---

### DMP-011. Transparency

The decisions generated by the automation shall be understandable to the user.

Whenever possible, the system shall provide the justification for each decision, indicating the primary criteria and rules that influenced the outcome.

---

### DMP-012. Rule Hierarchy

When two or more rules produce incompatible outcomes, the automation shall resolve the conflict by applying the priority order defined by the decision model.

The simultaneous execution of contradictory decisions for the same job posting shall not be permitted.

---

### DMP-013. Context Preservation

Every decision shall consider only the information relevant to the current state of the job posting and the context defined for the corresponding process.

Outdated, inconsistent, or information belonging to incompatible stages of the functional workflow shall not be used.

---

### DMP-014. No Autonomous Learning

The automation shall not autonomously modify business rules, evaluation criteria, decision thresholds, or any other element of the decision model.

Any modification shall require the user's explicit approval and the corresponding documentation.

---

### DMP-015. Consistency with the Functional Workflow

Every decision shall respect the functional workflow, the job posting lifecycle, and the state transitions defined for the automation.

No decision shall trigger incompatible transitions, skip mandatory stages, or alter the expected system behavior unless explicitly authorized by a documented rule.

---

# 3. Decision Model Architecture

The decision model architecture defines the conceptual structure through which the automation transforms the available information into consistent, traceable decisions aligned with the project's business rules.

The decision model is a cross-cutting component of the automation and may be used by different system modules without depending on any specific technological implementation.

Its operation is based on a sequential workflow consisting of inputs, evaluation, rule application, decision generation, and result recording.

---

## 3.1 Decision Model Components

The decision model shall consist of the following conceptual components:

### DMA-001. Inputs

Represents the set of information used to evaluate a job posting and determine the corresponding decision.

The inputs are defined in the "Decision Model Inputs" chapter of this document.

---

### DMA-002. Evaluation Engine

Component responsible for analyzing the available information and calculating the results required for decision-making.

Its responsibilities include:

- Analyzing evaluation criteria.
- Calculating scores.
- Detecting rule violations.
- Identifying special conditions.
- Generating intermediate results.

The evaluation engine does not make decisions; it only produces objective information for the next component.

---

### DMA-003. Rules Engine

Component responsible for applying the business rules defined for the project using the results generated by the evaluation engine.

Its responsibilities include:

- Applying acceptance rules.
- Applying rejection rules.
- Resolving conflicts between rules.
- Determining priorities.
- Identifying exceptions.
- Determining whether a decision can be executed automatically or requires user intervention.

---

### DMA-004. Decision Engine

Component responsible for issuing the final decision for each job posting.

The generated decisions shall comply with the principles defined in this document and remain aligned with the automation's functional workflow.

---

### DMA-005. Logging and Auditing

Component responsible for storing all information related to the decisions made.

At a minimum, it shall record:

- Evaluated data.
- Evaluation results.
- Applied rules.
- Decision obtained.
- Justification.
- Date and time.
- Decision owner.
- Resulting state.

---

## 3.2 Conceptual Decision Model Workflow

Every decision shall follow the following conceptual workflow:

1. Receive the required inputs for the process.
2. Validate the available information.
3. Evaluate the defined criteria.
4. Apply the business rules.
5. Resolve conflicts between rules, when applicable.
6. Determine the corresponding decision.
7. Assign the resulting state.
8. Fully record the decision to ensure traceability and auditability.

---

## 3.3 Decision Model Responsibilities

The decision model shall be responsible for:

- Objectively evaluating the available information.
- Applying the approved business rules.
- Issuing consistent and reproducible decisions.
- Prioritizing job postings according to the established criteria.
- Determining when a job posting should continue, stop, or require user intervention.
- Ensuring the traceability of all decisions.
- Preserving consistency with the functional workflow and the job posting lifecycle.

---

## 3.4 Responsibilities Outside the Scope

The decision model shall not be responsible for:

- Autonomously modifying business rules.
- Altering the user's professional profile.
- Replacing strategic decisions reserved for the user.
- Executing operational actions unrelated to the decision process.
- Implementing the technical logic of the modules that consume its decisions.
- Depending on a specific artificial intelligence model, programming language, or tool to produce its results.

---

# 4. Types of Decisions

The decision model classifies the automation's decisions according to their purpose, level of autonomy, impact on the functional workflow, and degree of user intervention.

This classification clearly defines the system's responsibilities and establishes the limits of the automation's authority.

---

## 4.1 Automated Decisions

Automated decisions are those that the automation may execute without user intervention, provided that previously documented business rules support their execution.

These decisions shall comply with all principles established in this document and maintain complete traceability.

Examples:

- Detect duplicate job postings.
- Validate mandatory information.
- Classify a job posting.
- Assign a priority.
- Reject a job posting for violating objective rules.
- Update functional workflow states.

---

### TD-001. Characteristics

Automated decisions shall be:

- Rule-based.
- Deterministic.
- Reproducible.
- Auditable.
- Reversible whenever permitted by the architecture.

---

## 4.2 Recommendations

Recommendations are conclusions generated by the automation to support the user's decision-making.

A recommendation does not by itself modify the state of a job posting or execute strategic actions.

Its purpose is to provide objective information that facilitates the user's final decision.

Examples:

- Recommend continuing with an application.
- Recommend manually reviewing a job posting.
- Recommend rejecting a job posting due to a low compatibility level.
- Recommend updating specific information before proceeding.

---

### TD-002. Characteristics

Recommendations shall:

- Be based on verifiable information.
- Be justified through documented rules.
- Maintain traceability.
- Be independent of the user's final decision.

---

## 4.3 Strategic Decisions

Strategic decisions are those whose impact exceeds the automation's responsibilities and therefore require the user's explicit authorization.

These decisions shall not be executed automatically.

Examples:

- Approve a job application.
- Decide to apply to a company.
- Modify professional criteria.
- Authorize the sharing of information with third parties.

---

### TD-003. Characteristics

Strategic decisions shall:

- Be made exclusively by the user.
- Be recorded as part of the job posting history.
- Preserve the corresponding justification.
- Maintain complete traceability.

---

## 4.4 Operational Decisions

Operational decisions control the internal operation of the automation and enable processing continuity.

These decisions affect the system's technical behavior but do not modify the user's strategic decisions.

Examples:

- Retry a process.
- Pause execution.
- Resume processing.
- Mark a job posting for review.
- Change the operational state of a job posting.

---

### TD-004. Characteristics

Operational decisions shall:

- Respect the defined recovery strategies.
- Preserve processing integrity.
- Maintain the consistency of the functional workflow.

---

## 4.5 Exception Decisions

Exception decisions correspond to situations outside the normal processing workflow that require the application of special rules.

Their objective is to preserve the stability and continuity of the automation whenever abnormal conditions occur.

Examples:

- Insufficient information.
- Conflicting rules.
- Inconsistent data.
- Unavailable external dependencies.
- Inconclusive results.

---

### TD-005. Characteristics

Exception decisions shall:

- Be applied only when a documented condition justifies them.
- Maintain complete traceability.
- Prioritize processing integrity.
- Request user intervention whenever no permitted automatic resolution exists.

---

## 4.6 Decision Hierarchy

When a single job posting is simultaneously subject to multiple decision types, the automation shall resolve them according to the following priority order:

1. Exception decisions.
2. User strategic decisions.
3. Automated decisions.
4. Operational decisions.
5. Recommendations.

No decision type may contradict another decision located at a higher level in this hierarchy.

---

## 4.7 Decision Uniqueness Principle

For the same functional process and the same job posting, there shall be only one active decision representing the final outcome of the decision process.

If multiple intermediate results are generated during evaluation, the decision model shall resolve them using the defined rules before issuing the final decision.

Any replaced, discarded, or modified decision shall be preserved in the history to ensure system traceability and auditability.

---

# 5. Decision Model Inputs

The decision model inputs correspond to the set of information that the automation may use to evaluate a job posting and determine the appropriate decision according to the defined business rules.

All inputs shall originate from valid sources, be properly identified, and have successfully passed the validation processes defined by the automation before being used for decision-making.

The decision model shall not use unvalidated, inconsistent, or contextually irrelevant information.

---

## DMI-001. Job Posting Information

Represents the structured information obtained during the job posting discovery and preparation processes.

It may include, among other items:

- Job title.
- Company.
- Description.
- Responsibilities.
- Requirements.
- Skills.
- Salary.
- Work arrangement.
- Location.
- Employment type.
- Required language.
- Publication date.
- Source platform.
- URL.
- Associated identifiers.

This information constitutes the primary input for the evaluation process.

---

## DMI-002. User Professional Profile

Represents the professional information used to measure compatibility between the user and the evaluated job posting.

It may include:

- Work experience.
- Technical skills.
- Professional skills.
- Academic background.
- Certifications.
- Languages.
- Job preferences.
- Salary expectations.
- Preferred work arrangement.
- Location.
- Target companies.
- Restricted companies.

The professional profile shall remain synchronized with the current version approved by the user.

---

## DMI-003. Business Rules

Represents the documented set of rules governing the behavior of the decision model.

It may include:

- Acceptance rules.
- Rejection rules.
- Prioritization rules.
- Classification rules.
- Thresholds.
- Exceptions.
- Constraints.

Business rules constitute the foundation of every automated decision.

---

## DMI-004. System Configuration

Represents the operational parameters that influence the behavior of the decision model.

It may include:

- General settings.
- Evaluation parameters.
- Configurable thresholds.
- Processing preferences.
- Module configurations.

Configurations shall be managed centrally.

---

## DMI-005. Historical Information

Represents the information generated during previous executions that is relevant to the current decision.

It may include:

- Job posting history.
- Previous evaluation results.
- Previous decisions.
- Reprocessing events.
- State changes.
- Relevant metrics.

The use of historical information shall never compromise the reproducibility of decisions.

---

## DMI-006. Intermediate Results

Represents the information produced by previous stages of the evaluation process and used as input for subsequent decisions.

It may include:

- Partial scores.
- Completed validations.
- Temporary classifications.
- Compatibility indicators.
- Observations generated during the evaluation.

These results may only be used within the context of the evaluated job posting.

---

## DMI-007. User Decisions

Represents the strategic decisions previously recorded by the user that must be considered during the processing of a job posting.

It may include:

- Approvals.
- Manual rejections.
- Priority modifications.
- Authorizations.
- Requested reprocessing.
- Approved changes to professional criteria.

These decisions shall take precedence over any recommendation generated by the automation whenever established by the system's rules.

---

## DMI-008. Job Posting State

Represents the current lifecycle state and operational state of the job posting at the time the decision process begins.

The state determines which rules may be applied and which decisions are permitted for that stage of the functional workflow.

Decisions incompatible with the current job posting state shall not be executed.

---

## General Principles for Inputs

Every input used by the decision model shall satisfy the following conditions:

- Be uniquely identifiable.
- Originate from a source authorized by the automation.
- Successfully pass the corresponding validation processes.
- Remain consistent with the current processing state.
- Be traceable throughout the entire job posting lifecycle.
- Remain available for audits and reprocessing whenever necessary.
- Comply with the integrity rules defined for the project.

---

# 6. Evaluation Criteria

Evaluation criteria represent the objective aspects that the decision model will analyze to determine the level of compatibility between a job posting and the user's professional profile.

Each criterion constitutes an independent evaluation dimension and will later be used by the scoring system and the decision rules.

The definition of the criteria does not establish their weight, relative importance, or acceptance thresholds. Those elements will be defined in the corresponding chapters of this document.

---

## EC-001. Professional Compatibility

Evaluates the degree of alignment between the user's professional profile and the profile required by the job posting.

It may consider, among other aspects:

- Job position.
- Professional field.
- Experience level.
- Seniority.
- Specialization.
- Primary responsibilities.

---

## EC-002. Technical Skills

Evaluates the level of alignment between the technical skills required by the job posting and those recorded in the user's professional profile.

It may consider:

- Technologies.
- Tools.
- Programming languages.
- Frameworks.
- Platforms.
- Methodologies.
- Specialized knowledge.

---

## EC-003. Professional Skills

Evaluates the alignment between the professional skills requested by the job posting and those recorded in the user's profile.

It may consider:

- Leadership.
- Communication.
- Teamwork.
- Organization.
- Problem-solving.
- Adaptability.
- Other relevant professional skills.

---

## EC-004. Work Experience

Evaluates the correspondence between the experience required by the job posting and the user's documented experience.

It may consider:

- Years of experience.
- Specific experience.
- Industries.
- Job responsibilities.
- Level of responsibility.
- Career trajectory.

---

## EC-005. Academic Background

Evaluates the compatibility between the education requested by the job posting and the education recorded in the professional profile.

It may consider:

- Academic level.
- Degrees.
- Educational programs.
- Specializations.
- Certifications.
- Additional studies.

---

## EC-006. Languages

Evaluates the alignment between the languages required by the job posting and the languages known by the user.

It may consider:

- Language.
- Required proficiency level.
- Demonstrated proficiency level.
- Language certifications.

---

## EC-007. Employment Conditions

Evaluates the compatibility between the conditions offered by the job posting and the user's employment preferences.

It may consider:

- Work arrangement.
- Employment type.
- Work schedule.
- Working hours.
- Required availability.

---

## EC-008. Geographic Location

Evaluates the compatibility between the job posting's location and the user's geographic preferences.

It may consider:

- City.
- Country.
- Remote work.
- Hybrid work.
- Relocation.
- Geographic restrictions.

---

## EC-009. Compensation

Evaluates the compatibility between the compensation offered and the salary expectations defined by the user.

It may consider:

- Salary.
- Salary range.
- Currency.
- Financial benefits, when relevant.

When the job posting does not include salary information, this criterion shall be handled according to the rules defined for incomplete information.

---

## EC-010. Company

Evaluates aspects related to the organization publishing the job posting.

It may consider:

- Target companies.
- Restricted companies.
- Industry sector.
- User-defined preferences.

---

## EC-011. Job Posting Quality

Evaluates the quality and sufficiency of the available information required to perform a reliable evaluation.

It may consider:

- Information completeness.
- Description clarity.
- Data consistency.
- Availability of mandatory information.
- Level of detail.

Low information quality may reduce the reliability of the evaluation without necessarily resulting in the rejection of the job posting.

---

## EC-012. User Constraints

Evaluates compliance with the constraints explicitly defined by the user for their job search.

It may consider, among others:

- Excluded companies.
- Undesired technologies.
- Unacceptable work arrangements.
- Restricted locations.
- Unacceptable employment conditions.
- Other user-configured constraints.

User-defined constraints shall take precedence over any compatibility criterion.

---

## General Evaluation Principles

All evaluation criteria shall satisfy the following conditions:

- Be evaluated independently.
- Be based exclusively on validated information.
- Be objective and reproducible.
- Maintain complete traceability.
- Use documented rules.
- Be extensible without affecting existing criteria.
- Remain independent of any specific technology or tool.
- Remain consistent with the user's professional profile and the current state of the job posting.

---

# 7. Scoring System

The scoring system defines the mechanism through which the decision model transforms the evaluation results into a quantitative measure of compatibility between a job posting and the user's professional profile.

Its purpose is to provide an objective, consistent, and reproducible assessment that serves as input for the acceptance, rejection, and prioritization rules.

The scoring system does not constitute a decision by itself; it represents only the numerical result obtained during the evaluation process.

---

## SS-001. Objective

The scoring system shall:

- Measure the compatibility level of each job posting.
- Facilitate comparisons between multiple job postings.
- Serve as input for the decision rules.
- Maintain consistent criteria across all evaluations.
- Reduce subjectivity during the evaluation process.

---

## SS-002. Scoring Structure

The score assigned to a job posting shall be composed of the combination of the results obtained from the evaluation criteria defined in this document.

Each criterion may contribute independently to the final score according to the established weighting rules.

The definition of the specific weights will be documented once the corresponding business rules have been designed.

---

## SS-003. Independence of Criteria

Each evaluation criterion shall be calculated independently before being integrated into the overall score.

The result obtained for one criterion shall not directly modify the calculation of another unless explicitly authorized by a documented rule.

---

## SS-004. Weighting

Each criterion may have a different relative weight in the calculation of the total score.

The weighting shall:

- Be documented.
- Be configurable.
- Be centrally managed.
- Be modifiable without altering the overall logic of the decision model.

---

## SS-005. Penalties

The system may apply penalties whenever a job posting fails to satisfy specific conditions defined by the business rules.

Penalties shall:

- Be documented in advance.
- Be objective.
- Be reproducible.
- Maintain traceability.

A penalty shall not necessarily result in the automatic rejection of a job posting.

---

## SS-006. Bonuses

The system may grant bonuses whenever a job posting presents characteristics that are especially favorable to the user's profile.

Bonuses shall:

- Be based on documented rules.
- Be objective.
- Be traceable.
- Maintain consistency across evaluations.

---

## SS-007. Insufficient Information

When one or more criteria cannot be evaluated because of missing information, the scoring system shall apply the treatment defined for incomplete information without generating inconsistent results.

Missing information shall not automatically be interpreted as either favorable or unfavorable unless explicitly determined by a specific rule.

---

## SS-008. Normalization

The final score shall be expressed using a uniform scale for all evaluated job postings.

The selected scale shall allow objective comparisons between different job opportunities.

---

## SS-009. Result Classification

Once the score has been calculated, the result shall be classified into a compatibility level.

The classification shall serve as input for the acceptance, rejection, and prioritization rules.

The specific classification ranges will be defined later together with the corresponding business rules.

---

## SS-010. Reproducibility

The score calculation shall always produce the same result whenever the following remain unchanged:

- The same inputs.
- The same rules.
- The same configuration.
- The same version of the decision model.

---

## SS-011. Traceability

Every generated score shall preserve the information required to fully reconstruct its calculation.

At a minimum, the following shall be recorded:

- Evaluated criteria.
- Individual results.
- Applied penalties.
- Applied bonuses.
- Final score.
- Obtained compatibility level.
- Date and time of the calculation.
- Version of the rules used.

---

## General Principles of the Scoring System

The scoring system shall comply with the following principles:

- Objectivity.
- Consistency.
- Transparency.
- Centralized configuration.
- Technology independence.
- Scalability.
- Auditability.
- Reproducibility.
- Traceability.
- Ease of maintenance.

---

# 8. Acceptance Rules

Acceptance rules establish the conditions that a job posting shall satisfy to continue advancing through the automation's functional workflow.

Their purpose is to ensure that only job postings with a sufficient level of compatibility and quality continue to the subsequent stages of processing.

Acceptance rules shall be applied after the evaluation process has been completed and before the deep processing of the job posting begins.

---

## AR-001. Compliance with Minimum Criteria

Every job posting shall satisfy the minimum criteria defined by the business rules in order to be accepted.

Failure to satisfy one or more mandatory criteria shall prevent acceptance, regardless of the score obtained.

---

## AR-002. Sufficient Compatibility

The job posting shall achieve the minimum compatibility level established by the scoring system.

The specific thresholds shall be defined in the corresponding business rules.

---

## AR-003. Information Integrity

The available information shall be sufficient to allow a reliable evaluation.

When the available information is insufficient, the automation shall apply the rules defined for incomplete information before accepting or rejecting the job posting.

---

## AR-004. Compliance with User Constraints

The job posting shall comply with every mandatory constraint defined by the user.

No job posting may be accepted if it violates a constraint classified as mandatory.

---

## AR-005. Information Consistency

The information used during the evaluation shall be free of inconsistencies that prevent the correct interpretation of the job posting.

Whenever significant inconsistencies are detected, the automation shall apply the corresponding validation or exception rules before continuing.

---

## AR-006. Valid State

A job posting may only be accepted if it is in a lifecycle state compatible with the evaluation process.

Job postings that have already completed processing, have been rejected, or are in states incompatible with evaluation shall not be accepted.

---

## AR-007. Absence of Conflicts

Before accepting a job posting, the decision model shall verify that no conflicts exist among the applied rules.

Whenever conflicts are detected, they shall be resolved according to the conflict resolution mechanism defined in this document before issuing the final decision.

---

## AR-008. Result Validation

A job posting may only be accepted after verifying that the evaluation has been completed successfully and that all required results are available.

Job postings with incomplete or interrupted evaluations shall not be accepted.

---

## AR-009. Mandatory Logging

Every acceptance decision shall be recorded as part of the job posting history.

At a minimum, the following information shall be preserved:

- Job posting identifier.
- Date and time.
- Evaluation result.
- Compatibility level.
- Applied rules.
- Acceptance justification.
- Assigned state.
- Decision owner.

---

## AR-010. Functional Workflow Continuity

The acceptance of a job posting authorizes only its transition to the next stage defined in the functional workflow.

Acceptance does not imply the automatic approval of a job application, the transmission of information to third parties, or any other strategic decision reserved for the user.

---

## General Principles of Acceptance

All acceptance rules shall comply with the following principles:

- Be based exclusively on documented rules.
- Be applied uniformly to all job postings.
- Be objective and reproducible.
- Maintain complete traceability.
- Respect user constraints.
- Preserve the integrity of the functional workflow.
- Allow subsequent audits.
- Remain independent of the technology used for their implementation.

---

# 9. Rejection Rules

Rejection rules establish the conditions under which a job posting shall terminate its processing without proceeding to the subsequent stages of the functional workflow.

Their purpose is to prevent unnecessary resource consumption on job postings that do not satisfy the minimum conditions defined by the decision model or that present restrictions incompatible with the user's professional profile.

Rejection rules shall be applied objectively, consistently, and only when supported by a documented business rule.

---

## RD-001. Failure to Meet Mandatory Criteria

Any job posting that fails to satisfy one or more criteria classified as mandatory shall be rejected.

Mandatory criteria shall be defined by the corresponding business rules.

---

## RD-002. Insufficient Compatibility

A job posting shall be rejected whenever the evaluation result indicates a compatibility level below the minimum threshold established for continuing the processing.

The specific threshold values shall be defined by the business rules.

---

## RD-003. Violation of Mandatory User Constraints

A job posting shall be rejected whenever it violates a user-defined constraint classified as mandatory.

Such constraints may include, among others:

- Restricted companies.
- Work arrangement.
- Location.
- Employment conditions.
- Unacceptable technologies.
- Other user-configured constraints.

---

## RD-004. Invalid Information

A job posting shall be rejected whenever the available information is inconsistent, corrupted, or insufficient to allow a reliable evaluation and no documented strategy exists to resolve the situation.

---

## RD-005. Duplicate Job Posting

Whenever a job posting is determined to correspond to a previously recorded opportunity and the business rules indicate that it should not be reprocessed, the system shall reject the new record while preserving the corresponding history.

Rejection due to duplication shall not result in the deletion of previously stored information.

---

## RD-006. Incompatible State

A job posting shall be rejected whenever it is in a lifecycle state that prevents further processing according to the defined functional workflow.

Completed, rejected, or otherwise incompatible job postings shall not be processed unless a previously authorized reprocessing rule exists.

---

## RD-007. Unresolvable Conflicts

Whenever conflicts between business rules are detected during evaluation and cannot be resolved through the mechanisms defined by the decision model, the job posting shall not continue automatically.

The system shall apply the strategy defined for these situations, which may include rejection or user intervention, as appropriate.

---

## RD-008. Rejection Validation

Before issuing a rejection decision, the automation shall verify that:

- The evaluation has been completed successfully.
- The applied rejection rule is valid.
- No higher-priority rule prevents the rejection.
- The result is consistent with the current state of the job posting.

---

## RD-009. Mandatory Logging

Every rejection decision shall be recorded as part of the job posting history.

At a minimum, the following information shall be preserved:

- Job posting identifier.
- Date and time.
- Applied rule.
- Reason for rejection.
- Evaluation results.
- Assigned state.
- Decision owner.

---

## RD-010. Processing Termination

A rejected job posting shall not continue to subsequent stages of the functional workflow.

It may only be processed again when a documented reprocessing rule exists or when explicitly authorized by the user.

---

## General Principles of Rejection

All rejection rules shall comply with the following principles:

- Be based exclusively on documented rules.
- Be applied uniformly to all job postings.
- Be objective and reproducible.
- Maintain complete traceability.
- Preserve the integrity of the history.
- Avoid rejections based on unvalidated information.
- Allow subsequent audits.
- Remain independent of the technology used for their implementation.

---

# 10. Job Posting Prioritization

Job posting prioritization defines the mechanism through which the automation orders accepted job opportunities according to their level of interest to the user.

Its purpose is to optimize the use of processing resources and facilitate decision-making by focusing efforts on the job postings that represent the greatest opportunity for success.

Prioritization may only be applied to job postings that have successfully passed the acceptance rules.

---

## JP-001. Prioritization Objective

Prioritization shall enable the system to:

- Determine the processing order of job postings.
- Identify the opportunities with the greatest potential.
- Optimize the use of automation resources.
- Facilitate user review.
- Establish a consistent classification criterion.

---

## JP-002. Prioritization Criteria

The priority assigned to a job posting may be determined using, among other factors:

- Scoring system result.
- Compatibility level.
- Compliance with key criteria.
- User constraints.
- Quality of the available information.
- Applicable business rules.
- Other criteria defined by the project.

The relative importance of each criterion shall be defined by the corresponding business rules.

---

## JP-003. Priority Levels

Every accepted job posting shall be assigned exactly one priority level.

The official priority levels of the decision model shall be:

- High priority.
- Medium priority.
- Low priority.

The specific criteria for assigning each level shall be documented in the business rules.

---

## JP-004. Priority Uniqueness

A job posting may have only one active priority level at any given time.

Whenever a new evaluation modifies the priority, the previous classification shall be preserved in the history to ensure traceability.

---

## JP-005. Reprioritization

The priority of a job posting may be recalculated whenever one of the following events occurs:

- Job posting information is updated.
- The user's professional profile is modified.
- Business rules are changed.
- Authorized reprocessing is performed.
- New relevant information becomes available.

Every reprioritization shall be recorded as a new event in the history.

---

## JP-006. Processing Independence

Priority determines the recommended order for processing or reviewing a job posting but does not by itself modify the evaluation result or replace the acceptance or rejection rules.

A high-priority job posting shall still comply with every rule defined by the functional workflow.

---

## JP-007. Tie Resolution

Whenever two or more job postings receive the same priority level, the decision model shall apply the tie-breaking mechanisms defined by the business rules.

Tie-breaking criteria shall be:

- Objective.
- Reproducible.
- Documented.
- Consistent.

Until those criteria are defined, job postings shall be considered equivalent within the same priority level.

---

## JP-008. Priority Validation

Before assigning a priority, the automation shall verify that:

- The evaluation has been completed successfully.
- A valid score exists.
- All corresponding rules have been applied.
- No unresolved conflicts remain.
- The job posting remains eligible for processing.

---

## JP-009. Prioritization Logging

Every priority assignment or modification shall be recorded as part of the job posting history.

At a minimum, the following information shall be preserved:

- Job posting identifier.
- Date and time.
- Assigned priority level.
- Evaluation result.
- Applied rules.
- Classification justification.
- Decision owner.

---

## JP-010. Priority Usage

Priority may be used as a criterion to:

- Determine the order of deep processing.
- Organize the job postings presented to the user.
- Schedule future executions.
- Optimize resource allocation.
- Generate reports and statistics.

Priority does not automatically authorize any strategic action reserved for the user.

---

## General Principles of Prioritization

Job posting prioritization shall comply with the following principles:

- Be based exclusively on documented rules.
- Maintain consistency across equivalent evaluations.
- Be objective and reproducible.
- Preserve complete traceability.
- Allow priority recalculation whenever the evaluation context changes.
- Remain independent of the technology used for implementation.
- Facilitate the scalability of the decision model.

---

# 11. Decision Transition Rules

Decision transition rules establish the conditions under which a decision may modify the state of a job posting within the automation's functional workflow.

Their purpose is to ensure that every transition remains consistent with the decision model, the job posting lifecycle, and the business rules defined for the project.

No decision shall produce state changes that have not been previously documented and authorized by this model.

---

## DTR-001. Authorized Transitions

Every decision shall produce only transitions that have been previously defined within the automation's functional workflow.

Implicit, arbitrary, or undocumented state transitions shall not be permitted.

---

## DTR-002. Prior Validation

Before executing a transition, the automation shall verify that:

- The corresponding evaluation has been completed successfully.
- The decision has been fully determined.
- No unresolved conflicts remain.
- The job posting is in a state compatible with the requested transition.

---

## DTR-003. State Compatibility

Transitions may occur only between compatible lifecycle states and operational states of the job posting.

Transitions that contradict the approved functional workflow shall not be permitted.

---

## DTR-004. Acceptance Transitions

Whenever a job posting satisfies the acceptance rules, the resulting decision shall allow it to advance to the next stage defined within the functional workflow.

Acceptance of a job posting does not automatically authorize any strategic action reserved for the user.

---

## DTR-005. Rejection Transitions

Whenever a job posting fails to satisfy the acceptance rules or satisfies a rejection rule, the automation shall terminate its processing according to the defined functional workflow.

The rejection shall preserve all information required to support audits and, whenever applicable, future reprocessing.

---

## DTR-006. User Intervention Transitions

Whenever a strategic decision requires user approval, the automation shall suspend the automatic transition until the corresponding user decision is received.

Once the user's decision has been recorded, the workflow shall continue according to the rules defined for that scenario.

---

## DTR-007. Reprocessing Transitions

Whenever a job posting is authorized for reprocessing, the automation may return only to stages previously defined by the business rules.

Reprocessing shall not delete or overwrite the history of previous decisions.

---

## DTR-008. Recovery Transitions

Whenever an interrupted process is successfully recovered, the automation shall continue from the most appropriate state, avoiding the repetition of stages that have already been completed successfully.

Every recovery shall comply with the defined failure recovery strategies.

---

## DTR-009. Restriction of Incompatible Transitions

The following transitions, among others, shall not be permitted:

- Skipping mandatory stages.
- Arbitrarily moving backward through the functional workflow.
- Executing stages out of sequence.
- Simultaneously maintaining incompatible states.
- Modifying final states without explicit authorization.

Any exception shall be supported by a previously documented business rule.

---

## DTR-010. Transition Logging

Every transition shall be recorded as part of the job posting history.

At a minimum, the following information shall be preserved:

- Previous state.
- Resulting state.
- Decision that triggered the transition.
- Applied rule.
- Date and time.
- Decision owner.
- Transition justification.

---

## General Decision Transition Workflow

The decision model shall follow the following conceptual workflow to determine the corresponding transition:

1. Verify the current state of the job posting.
2. Validate that the required information is available.
3. Apply the evaluation rules.
4. Determine the corresponding decision.
5. Verify transition compatibility.
6. Update the job posting state.
7. Record the transition in the history.
8. Continue with the next functional process or terminate processing, as appropriate.

---

## General Principles of Decision Transitions

All decision transitions shall comply with the following principles:

- Be based exclusively on documented rules.
- Maintain consistency with the functional workflow.
- Preserve the integrity of the job posting lifecycle.
- Be objective and reproducible.
- Maintain complete traceability.
- Avoid inconsistent states.
- Allow subsequent audits.
- Remain independent of the technology used for implementation.

---

# 12. Handling Special Cases

Special cases correspond to situations anticipated by the decision model that, due to their particular characteristics, require treatment different from the normal evaluation workflow without necessarily constituting a system error or exception.

Their purpose is to preserve decision consistency, reduce unnecessary user intervention, and ensure that job postings are processed according to previously documented rules.

Every special case shall have a defined handling strategy before being incorporated into the decision model.

---

## SC-001. Incomplete Information

Whenever a job posting does not contain all the information required to evaluate one or more criteria, the automation shall apply the rules defined for incomplete information.

The handling strategy shall depend on the importance of the missing information and the corresponding business rules.

Missing information shall not automatically result in either acceptance or rejection of the job posting.

---

## SC-002. Conflicting Information

Whenever the information contained in a job posting is inconsistent across its different fields or sources, the automation shall identify the conflict and apply the corresponding validation rules before issuing a decision.

As long as the conflict remains unresolved, no final decision shall be generated.

---

## SC-003. Updated Information

Whenever a previously recorded job posting receives new relevant information, the system shall determine whether the update requires a new evaluation or only an update to the history.

Every reevaluation shall preserve the traceability of previous decisions.

---

## SC-004. Previously Processed Job Posting

Whenever a job posting has already been processed, the decision model shall determine whether it is appropriate to:

- Keep the existing decision.
- Update the evaluation.
- Reprioritize the job posting.
- Reprocess it.
- Preserve only the history.

The decision shall depend on the reprocessing rules defined for the project.

---

## SC-005. Multiple Locations

Whenever a job posting includes multiple locations or work arrangements, the automation shall evaluate them according to the user's preferences and constraints before issuing a decision.

---

## SC-006. Missing Salary Information

Whenever a job posting does not include compensation information, the corresponding evaluation criterion shall be handled according to the rules defined for incomplete information.

The absence of salary information shall not automatically result in the rejection of the job posting.

---

## SC-007. Partially Applicable Rules

Whenever one or more evaluation criteria are not applicable to a specific job posting, the system shall exclude those criteria from the evaluation process according to the defined business rules while preserving the consistency of the overall result.

---

## SC-008. Changes to Business Rules

Whenever business rules are modified after a job posting has been evaluated, the decision model shall determine whether to preserve the current decision or initiate a reevaluation process.

Every reevaluation shall be recorded as a new event in the history.

---

## SC-009. User Intervention During Evaluation

Whenever the user intervenes before the decision process has been completed, the automation shall record the intervention and adapt the workflow according to the corresponding rules.

User decisions shall take precedence over automated recommendations whenever established by the decision model.

---

## SC-010. Future Special Cases

The addition of new special cases shall be carried out through documented rules while preserving compatibility with the existing decision model.

No special case may modify the general principles defined in this document.

---

## General Principles for Handling Special Cases

Every special case shall comply with the following principles:

- Be documented in advance.
- Have objective identification criteria.
- Include a defined handling strategy.
- Maintain complete processing traceability.
- Preserve the consistency of the decision model.
- Avoid ambiguous or contradictory decisions.
- Not alter the functional workflow unless explicitly authorized by a documented rule.
- Facilitate the addition of new special cases without affecting existing ones.

---

# 13. Exception Handling

Exception handling defines the rules that the decision model shall apply whenever a situation prevents the normal evaluation process from continuing and cannot be resolved through the standard functional workflow.

Its purpose is to preserve processing integrity, ensure decision consistency, and minimize the operational impact of unexpected or abnormal situations.

Every exception shall be detected, recorded, handled, and resolved according to the strategies defined in this document.

---

## EH-001. Exception Identification

The decision model shall identify any situation that prevents a valid decision from being determined using the system's normal rules.

The identification of an exception does not necessarily imply a system error.

---

## EH-002. Exception Classification

Every exception shall be classified according to its nature before applying a resolution strategy.

Exceptions may originate from, among other causes:

- Insufficient information.
- Inconsistent information.
- Conflicts between rules.
- Incompatible states.
- External dependencies.
- Evaluation errors.
- Invalid configurations.

The specific classification shall be documented in the project's Error Handling document.

---

## EH-003. Recoverability Assessment

Once an exception has been identified, the automation shall determine whether it can be resolved automatically or requires user intervention.

The selected strategy shall comply with the business rules and preserve processing consistency.

---

## EH-004. Automatic Resolution

Whenever a documented strategy exists for resolving an exception, the automation may execute it autonomously.

Automatic resolution shall:

- Comply with the decision model rules.
- Preserve existing information.
- Maintain complete traceability.
- Avoid side effects on other job postings.

---

## EH-005. Escalation to the User

Whenever an exception cannot be resolved automatically or involves a strategic decision, the automation shall request user intervention before continuing.

During this period, the job posting shall remain in a state compatible with waiting for that decision.

---

## EH-006. Context Preservation

Every exception shall preserve the complete context in which it occurred.

At a minimum, the following information shall remain available:

- Job posting state.
- Evaluated information.
- Applied rules.
- Obtained results.
- Time at which the exception occurred.

This information shall enable analysis, auditing, and future reprocessing.

---

## EH-007. Processing Continuity

Once the exception has been resolved, the automation shall continue processing from the most appropriate stage, avoiding the repetition of stages that have already been completed successfully.

Whenever continuation is not possible, the job posting shall follow the workflow defined for termination or user intervention.

---

## EH-008. Integrity Protection

No exception shall result in:

- Information loss.
- Data corruption.
- Incompatible states.
- Contradictory decisions.
- Omission of mandatory records.

Information integrity shall take precedence over processing continuity.

---

## EH-009. Mandatory Logging

Every exception shall be recorded as part of the job posting history.

At a minimum, the following information shall be preserved:

- Job posting identifier.
- Exception type.
- Description.
- Date and time.
- State in which the exception occurred.
- Involved rule, when applicable.
- Applied strategy.
- Obtained result.
- Resolution owner.

---

## EH-010. Continuous Improvement

The information recorded about exceptions shall be used to identify opportunities for improving the decision model.

The incorporation of new resolution strategies shall require updating the project's official documentation before implementation.

---

## General Principles for Exception Handling

Exception handling shall comply with the following principles:

- Detect abnormal situations promptly.
- Preserve information integrity.
- Maintain processing continuity whenever possible.
- Escalate only those decisions that cannot be resolved automatically.
- Maintain complete traceability.
- Guarantee the reproducibility of the applied strategies.
- Avoid improvised or undocumented decisions.
- Remain independent of the technology used for implementation.

---

# 14. User-Reserved Decisions

User-reserved decisions are those that, due to their strategic, personal, professional, or legal nature, shall not be executed automatically by the automation.

Their purpose is to preserve the user's control over the highest-impact decisions, ensuring that the automation acts as a support system rather than a replacement for human judgment.

No business rule, automated process, or system component may modify this principle without the user's explicit approval and the corresponding update to the project's official documentation.

---

## URD-001. Approval of a Job Opportunity

The decision to consider a job posting as a worthwhile opportunity shall be the exclusive responsibility of the user.

The automation may generate recommendations, analyses, and scores, but it shall not decide on behalf of the user whether a job posting represents a professional opportunity.

---

## URD-002. Rejection Based on Personal Criteria

The user may reject a job posting for personal, strategic, or any other reasons that cannot be determined objectively through the system's rules.

Such reasons may include, among others:

- Personal interest.
- Organizational culture.
- Company reputation.
- Previous experiences.
- Professional preferences.
- Information obtained outside the automation.

---

## URD-003. Priority Modification

The user may modify the priority automatically assigned to a job posting whenever strategic reasons justify doing so.

Every modification shall preserve the original value calculated by the automation as part of the history.

---

## URD-004. Job Application Approval

The decision to prepare a final application for a job posting shall be the exclusive responsibility of the user.

The automation may generate all the information required to facilitate that decision but shall never make it automatically.

---

## URD-005. Authorization to Share Information

Any action involving the transmission, publication, or sharing of the user's personal or professional information with third parties shall require explicit authorization.

Such actions include, among others:

- Sending a résumé.
- Sending a portfolio.
- Completing application forms.
- Sending emails.
- Sharing documents.

---

## URD-006. Professional Profile Modification

Every modification related to the user's professional profile shall require the user's explicit approval.

This includes, among other elements:

- Résumé.
- Professional profile.
- Portfolio.
- Work experience.
- Skills.
- Certifications.
- Employment preferences.

The automation may propose changes but shall never apply them automatically.

---

## URD-007. Business Rule Modification

Every modification to the decision model shall require user authorization.

This includes, among others:

- Evaluation criteria.
- Acceptance rules.
- Rejection rules.
- Scoring system.
- Thresholds.
- Prioritization.
- Special cases.
- Exceptions.

No system component may modify these rules autonomously.

---

## URD-008. Exceptional Reprocessing

Whenever a job posting requires reprocessing that cannot be justified by the existing automated rules, the decision shall be made by the user.

The authorization shall be recorded together with the corresponding justification.

---

## URD-009. Addition of New Job Sources

The decision to incorporate a new job source into the system shall belong exclusively to the user.

The automation shall not integrate new platforms, APIs, or websites on its own initiative.

---

## URD-010. Addition of New Features

Any functional expansion of the automation shall require the user's prior approval before implementation.

This includes any modification that changes the originally approved functional scope of the project.

---

## General Principles of User-Reserved Decisions

All user-reserved decisions shall comply with the following principles:

- Require explicit authorization before execution.
- Maintain complete traceability.
- Preserve the history of all decisions made.
- Take precedence over recommendations generated by the automation whenever a conflict exists.
- Not be automated without a formal modification of the project documentation.
- Preserve the user's autonomy over strategic decisions.
- Remain independent of the technology used to implement the automation.

---

# 15. Traceability and Auditing

Traceability and auditing define the mechanisms through which the decision model preserves the evidence required to reconstruct, verify, analyze, and validate every decision made during the processing of job postings.

Their purpose is to ensure transparency, reproducibility, accountability, and continuous improvement of the automation while maintaining consistency with the project's Functional Requirements, Non-Functional Requirements, and Quality Principles.

All decisions generated by the automation shall be fully traceable from their origin to the final state reached by the corresponding job posting.

---

## TA-001. Complete Decision Traceability

Every decision generated by the automation shall preserve sufficient information to allow the complete reconstruction of the decision-making process.

Traceability shall cover every stage of the evaluation, rule application, transition, and final result.

---

## TA-002. Mandatory Audit Information

Every recorded decision shall preserve, at a minimum, the following information:

- Job posting identifier.
- Execution identifier.
- Decision identifier.
- Date and time.
- Current job posting state.
- Previous state.
- Resulting state.
- Evaluation results.
- Applied rules.
- Generated score.
- Assigned compatibility level.
- Assigned priority.
- Decision justification.
- Decision owner.
- Version of the business rules.
- Version of the decision model.

Additional information may be incorporated provided that it does not compromise the consistency of the recorded information.

---

## TA-003. Immutability of Historical Records

Historical decision records shall remain immutable.

Whenever a decision changes, the automation shall create a new historical record without modifying or deleting the previous one.

This principle ensures complete auditability throughout the entire job posting lifecycle.

---

## TA-004. Decision History

Every job posting shall maintain a complete history of all relevant events generated during its processing.

The history may include, among others:

- Evaluations.
- Decisions.
- State transitions.
- Reprocessing events.
- User interventions.
- Exceptions.
- Priority changes.
- Business rule updates.
- Recoveries.

---

## TA-005. Rule Traceability

Every decision shall preserve the exact business rules used during its evaluation.

Whenever the rules evolve over time, the recorded version shall allow the decision to be reproduced under the same historical conditions.

---

## TA-006. Evaluation Traceability

The system shall preserve the intermediate results produced during the evaluation whenever they are necessary to reconstruct the final decision.

Intermediate information may include:

- Partial scores.
- Individual criterion results.
- Validation outcomes.
- Applied penalties.
- Applied bonuses.
- Compatibility calculations.

---

## TA-007. User Action Traceability

Every user intervention affecting the decision model shall be recorded.

This includes, among others:

- Manual approvals.
- Manual rejections.
- Priority changes.
- Rule modifications.
- Reprocessing authorizations.
- Professional profile updates.

User interventions shall take precedence over automated recommendations whenever established by the project's business rules.

---

## TA-008. Audit Availability

Audit information shall remain available throughout the lifecycle defined by the project's data retention policies.

The storage mechanism shall ensure the integrity, accessibility, and consistency of historical records.

---

## TA-009. Reproducibility

The recorded information shall allow any decision to be reproduced whenever the following remain unchanged:

- The same inputs.
- The same configuration.
- The same business rules.
- The same version of the decision model.

The reproduced result shall be equivalent to the original decision.

---

## TA-010. Continuous Improvement

The information generated through traceability and auditing shall support:

- Process analysis.
- Detection of improvement opportunities.
- Validation of new business rules.
- Identification of recurring situations.
- Continuous optimization of the decision model.

Any improvement incorporated into the system shall be documented before implementation.

---

## General Principles of Traceability and Auditing

Traceability and auditing shall comply with the following principles:

- Preserve the complete history of every decision.
- Guarantee information integrity.
- Allow full decision reconstruction.
- Facilitate internal and external audits.
- Preserve the reproducibility of historical decisions.
- Maintain transparency throughout the decision-making process.
- Remain independent of the technology used for implementation.
- Support the long-term evolution of the decision model without compromising historical consistency.
